Past Simple

Choose a form to learn and practise.

1. When do we use it?

We use the Past Simple for an action that started and finished in the past.

Alice played tennis yesterday.

George visited his grandparents last weekend.

2. Sentence Form

Subject + V2 + ...

V2 is the past form of a verb. Regular and irregular verbs use the same sentence form. Their V2 forms are different.

Oliver visited his grandparents yesterday. Regular

Daisy went to school by bus. Irregular

The V2 form stays the same with different subjects:
I played. She played. They played.
The verb be is different: I / he / she / it was; you / we / they were.

3. Regular Verbs

We usually add -ed to make the V2 form of a regular verb.

play → playedwatch → watchedclean → cleanedvisit → visited

Oliver cleaned his room yesterday.

Grace watched a film last night.

4. Regular Verb Spelling

Most verbs: add -ed
watch → watched
clean → cleaned
visit → visited
pack → packed
Ends in -e: add -d
dance → danced
live → lived
like → liked
smile → smiled
Consonant + y: change y to i, add -ed
study → studied
carry → carried
try → tried
cry → cried
Vowel + y: add -ed
play → played
stay → stayed
enjoy → enjoyed
Some short verbs: double the last letter, add -ed
stop → stopped
plan → planned
hop → hopped

5. Irregular Verbs

Irregular verbs do not make V2 by adding -ed. Learn each V2 form separately.

go → wenthave → hadeat → atesee → sawdo → didfeed → fedfeel → felt

Arthur went to the park yesterday.

Daisy had breakfast at seven.

William ate an apple after school.

Oliver fed his pet yesterday morning.

Lily felt excited about the school trip.

6. Time Expressions

These words tell us about time in the past.

yesterdayyesterday morninglast nightlast weekendlast weektwo days ago

Charlie watched a film last night.

Sarah visited her grandparents last weekend.

1. When do we use it?

We use Past Simple negative sentences to talk about actions that did not happen in the past.

Sarah didn’t play tennis yesterday.

Michael didn’t visit his grandparents last weekend.

2. Sentence Form

Subject + didn’t + V1 + ...

didn’t = did not
V1 = the base form of the verb

Tom didn’t watch a film last night.

Sarah didn’t go to school by bus yesterday.

They didn’t have breakfast at home.

Use didn’t with every subject:
I / you / he / she / it / we / they didn’t play.

3. Remember: Use V1!

After didn’t, use V1, not V2.
✓ Michael didn’t play football.
✗ Michael didn’t played football.
✓ Alice didn’t go to the park.
✗ Alice didn’t went to the park.

Use didn’t + V1 with regular and irregular action verbs.

Be is different: use wasn’t / weren’t with be. For example: She wasn’t tired. They weren’t tired.

4. Positive → Negative

Change the verb to didn’t + V1.

Tom played football yesterday.
Tom didn’t play football yesterday.

Sarah went to school by bus.
Sarah didn’t go to school by bus.

Michael fed his pet in the morning.
Michael didn’t feed his pet in the morning.

They had breakfast at home.
They didn’t have breakfast at home.

1. Yes/No Questions

Use a Yes/No question to ask if an action happened in the past.

Question Form

Did + subject + V1 + ...?

Did Tom play football yesterday?

Did Sarah go to school by bus?

Did Michael feed his pet?

Short Answers

Yes, I / you / he / she / it / we / they did.
No, I / you / he / she / it / we / they didn’t.

Did Sarah go to school by bus?
Yes, she did. / No, she didn’t.

Did Tom and Michael play football?
Yes, they did. / No, they didn’t.

In a short answer, do not repeat the main verb.

2. Wh- Questions

Use what, where, when and who to ask for information.

Question Form

Wh- word + did + subject + V1 + ...?

What did Tom watch last night?
He watched a film.

Where did Sarah go yesterday?
She went to the park.

When did Michael visit his grandparents?
He visited them last weekend.

Who did Alice visit yesterday?
She visited her grandmother.

Give the information in your answer, not just yes or no.

3. Remember: Did + V1

After did, use V1, not V2.
✓ Did Sarah go to school?
✗ Did Sarah went to school?
✓ Where did Tom play?
✗ Where did Tom played?

Use did + V1 with regular and irregular action verbs.

Be is different: ask Was she ...? or Were they ...?
