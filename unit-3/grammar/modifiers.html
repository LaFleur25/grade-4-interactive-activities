Modifiers for Adjectives

Read the explanations and examples.

1. What is a modifier?

An adjective describes a person or thing, like bright, big or beautiful. A modifier tells us how strong or weak that description is.

modifier + adjective
very + bright → very bright

Put the modifier before the adjective.

The painting is very bright.

2. Meet the modifiers

🔴 Too

Too means more than we want or need. There is a problem.

The box is too heavy. I can’t carry it.

🔵 Very / Really

Very and really make an adjective stronger. They do not always mean there is a problem.

The painting is very colourful.
The sculpture is really modern.

🟣 Pretty

Pretty means quite or fairly. It makes the adjective less strong than very.

The picture is pretty clear.

🟢 Not very

Not very means only a little. The description is weak.

The photo is not very clear. It is blurry.

3. Very or too?

🎒 The bag is very heavy.
It is heavy, but I can carry it.

🎒 The bag is too heavy.
It is a problem: I can’t carry it.

Remember: too tells us there is more than we want or need.

4. Look at the word order

Subject + be + modifier + adjective

The room is too dark.

The paintings are really bright.

We can also use a modifier before an adjective and a noun:

It is a very colourful picture.
